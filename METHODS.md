# Method

*Luck Surface Area_Revised* (v.01.01) · Post-Industrial School

## Question

Roberts' formula, L = D × T, treats telling as a quantity. This research asks whether the environment telling happens in changes how much luck it returns, and if so, what properties of an environment matter.

## Design

An exploratory survey of founders, read against prior quantitative research on proximity, weak ties and network position. The survey is treated as a qualitative founder panel: it shows where founders say their time goes and what they credit, not a representative measurement.

- **Participants:** eight founders in Vancouver's startup ecosystem.
- **Recruitment:** the survey link was shared with local contacts, posted on LinkedIn and distributed at the Startup Vancouver conference.
- **Period:** April 2026.
- **Format:** short remote survey, completed online.
- **Ethics:** approved under the Langara College ARC Research Fund.

## What was asked

Questions were grouped in the clusters below.

| Cluster | Topic |
|---|---|
| Time allocation | Hours per month spent in each of four environment types: social platforms, physical coworking, online communities, structured events |
| Serendipity rating | How much serendipity each environment returns, on a 1 to 5 scale |
| Attribution | Where unexpected opportunities (referrals, hires, clients, introductions) started |
| Same effort, different luck | Whether equal effort in different environments returned different results |
| Pullback | Which environments founders reduce or abandon, and why |
| Local advice | Where founders would tell others to put networking energy |

## Analysis

Responses on hours and ratings were aggregated across the eight founders for each environment type (see `data/environments.csv`). Open responses were read for patterns and used qualitatively. No statistical testing was done; with n = 8 the figures describe this group, not founders in general.

The founder data was then positioned against prior research with far larger samples: open-plan office studies, the LinkedIn weak-ties experiment (20 million users) and Burt's study of 673 managers. See `references.bib`.

## Limits

- **Small sample.** Eight founders. The pattern is exploratory, not representative.
- **Self-report.** Hours and ratings are what founders reported and perceived, not measured outcomes.
- **Perceived serendipity.** The 1 to 5 rating measures how much serendipity founders credit to an environment, not a count of opportunities.
- **Mechanism is argued, not tested.** The three properties (constraint, frame, friction) are proposed criteria drawn from the data and prior research. They have not been tested experimentally.

## Claim layers

As set out in section 08 of the essay:

- **Supported:** proximity alone is not the mechanism.
- **Inferred:** founders have a revealed preference, not a stated one.
- **Argued:** constraint, frame and friction are the criteria, and they are portable.

## Privacy

Only aggregate figures are published. Individual responses are not, to protect participants.
